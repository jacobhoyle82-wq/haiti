Why gangs rose in Haiti

Inkstick Contributor 2024, Inside Haiti’s Evolving Gang Landscape, Inkstick, viewed 22 August 2026, <https://inkstickmedia.com/inside-haitis-evolving-gang-landscape/>.

  • Weak and unstable governments created a power vacuum that gangs were able to exploit. The 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moïse further weakened the state.
  • Police have struggled to control heavily organised gangs, while corruption and a lack of effective justice have allowed criminals to act with significant impunity.
  • High poverty, inequality and a lack of employment opportunities have made gang recruitment easier, particularly among disadvantaged young people.
  • Some gangs have historically had relationships with political and economic actors, helping them gain influence and resources.
  • Armed groups have existed in parts of Haiti for decades, particularly in areas where the government has had little presence. Over time, some became larger and more organised criminal networks.

How gangs are fueled

  • Gangs demand money from businesses, residents and people using roads or other areas they control.
  • Ransom payments provide another source of income.
  • Criminal markets generate significant profits that can be reinvested into gang operations.
  • Large numbers of illegal firearms and ammunition enter Haiti, giving gangs the ability to maintain and expand their power.
  • By controlling roads, ports and neighbourhoods, gangs can collect money from local communities and control important economic activity.
